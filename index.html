I'm Tornike. I'm a builder from Tbilisi, Georgia.

For the past two years I've been making software that runs on AI — connecting models, designing systems, shipping real products that real people use. But somewhere along the way I realized something: I've been working on the engine without knowing how to steer it.

I know how to make AI do things. I don't yet know how to make those things mean something.

That's why I'm here.

01

Know-It-All Bot

AI & Creative

A quiz app that runs on three different AI models simultaneously — Groq, Gemini, and Cohere. Each one has a different speed, a different personality, a different way of thinking about a question.

But that's not the interesting part.

The interesting part is that I could make it about anything. Any topic, any difficulty, any format I could imagine. I realized I wasn't just building software — I was building a universe with unlimited scenarios. Whatever I could think of, I could create it for any user.

One evening someone played it and laughed at exactly the right moment, got frustrated, came back for another round. That's the moment I stopped thinking like an engineer and started thinking like someone who makes experiences.

02

GTU Hackathon

Solo · 2nd Place

8 hours. No team. One constraint I set for myself: a stranger had to understand it in 30 seconds.

I built a platform where any user can create their own quiz game, and anyone in the world can play it. The full system — database, backend, frontend — built alone while others had teams of three or four.

Looking back, the technical part was the easy part. The hard part was deciding what to cut — what not to build. That's a creative decision, and I didn't have a word for it at the time.

EV Distributed System

University of Alicante · ERASMUS+

Built during my ERASMUS+ year in Spain. A fully distributed electric vehicle charging system — five components talking to each other, handling over 100 simultaneous users.

I built this in Alicante, in Spanish, surrounded by people from across Europe. Partway through, my teammate stepped back. I rebuilt the project from scratch, alone, in the final 10 days.

That environment changed how I think. Not just what I know — how I think. Spain is where I work best. I know that now.
